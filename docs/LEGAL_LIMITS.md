# Legal Limits

NaariSaakshi helps a person keep an organised, dated record and draft complaint documents. It is **not legal advice**, and it cannot make any record admissible or persuasive. Those decisions belong to an Internal/Local Committee, the police, or a court.

This page lists what the app says about Indian law, the source for each statement, and what it deliberately does not claim. Anything legal in the UI should trace back to a row here.

_Last reviewed: 2026-10-11. Statutes change; re-check before relying on this._

## What the app does not claim

- **That a record is "court-ready", "admissible" or "verified".** Section 63 certificates must be completed and signed by the appropriate person and expert. The app can record hashes and prepare fields, but it cannot certify.
- **That recording any conversation you are part of is lawful.** *Malkani* concerns admissibility. Privacy, workplace policy and the setting all matter. The app tells users to record only conversations they are part of, never in private spaces, and to stop if recording puts them at risk.
- **That a timestamp label on a photo proves when it was taken.** It is a visible label for readability.
- **That a SHA-256 hash proves an event happened.** A hash shows that a file has not changed since it was hashed. It says nothing about whether the content is true or when it was first created.
- **Which offence applies.** Mapping conduct to BNS or IT Act sections is a question for a lawyer or the police. Any future mapping will be framed as "provisions to discuss with a lawyer", never as an outcome.
- **That the pattern score measures danger.** It is a frequency summary of the user's own entries.
